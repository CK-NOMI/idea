## T1 — exact fixed-version aggregation; early-send obstruction
Fixed precommitted cohort, model snapshot and weights, additive gradients G_j released at r_j, common final update deadline d>=max r_j. No consuming partial aggregates before d, no dropping/HT, no intervening optimizer update. One authorized shared-link repair transaction at each exogenous opportunity time; gradient payload must be ready and fit (ideal unlimited capacity / zero additional serialization delay for counting), no hidden privacy authorization. Same-version summed gradient G=sum_j G_j regardless of partition into uploads; identical deterministic optimizer update in exact real arithmetic, fixed floating reduction tree or tolerance needed for finite precision. If all contributions use same vector space and each transmitted partial aggregate costs one dense vector, any valid strategy must send at least one vector at or after last release R=max r_j (unless last contribution known identically zero, excluded by required delivery). If no repair in [R,d], at least one paid activation required. Waiting to R, sending the entire vector at first repair thereafter, or at d otherwise, attains both minima simultaneously: exactly one vector and paid activation 1{no repair in [R,d]}. Greedy earlier piggyback cannot reduce paid activations and can increase full-vector transmissions, but can reduce average waiting, and with finite bandwidth/compute overlap can improve makespan; theorem not for those cases. Fixed deadline preserves round count, not actual latency/staleness under different deadlines. For distinct items each having its own interval [r_j,d_j], unlimited capacity and no per-payload cost, send all currently pending at every mandatory repair, and at first pending deadline when no repair. Pathwise optimal paid count: remove intervals containing free repair times; cover remaining intervals by smallest right endpoint repeatedly (standard interval stabbing). Online greedy agrees because first paid activation corresponds to an uncovered earliest-ending interval, any later ready items it clears can also be covered then; repeat. 600 synthetic finite interval cases against exhaustive subsets, zero differences. Generic batching theorem, not new foundational math or unique HFL.
## T2 — repair/learning feedback and exact deadline policy
R81 m=1 (independent SD) or m=4 (2-bit rank relay). At every fixed causal frontier choose protocol from previous event history and ready/upload status BEFORE fresh local drafts, flag, verifier RNG; conditional local target product law same for either protocol, hence adaptive selection preserves formal law by iterated conditioning. This does not permit post-draft mode switching or arbitrary future-lookahead selection; full multi-token EOS/greedy/top-p/FP not implemented. Let r0_t>=r1_t be exact repair activation probabilities for independent/coupled group; cost b>0 per mandatory group repair start, c>=0 additional coordinated control cost per slot, fallback standalone gradient start also b. Normalize by b, k=c/b. Fixed one-vector cost independent of strategy. Once full gradient is ready, pending u=1 or already sent u=0. Let V_t^0 be optimal expected future cost when sent, V_t^1 when pending, terminal V_{T+1}^0=0,V_{T+1}^1=1. Future context and rate schedule fixed exogenous at current snapshot, conditional fresh draws; no time budget change by chosen mode. V_t^0=V_(t+1)^0+min_a(r_a+k a); V_t^1=V_(t+1)^0+min_a[r_a+k a+(1-r_a)D_(t+1)], D_t=V_t^1−V_t^0. Before gradient ready use u=0 actions but no upload until fixed R. By backward induction D_t in [0,1]: pending cannot reduce repair/control optimum; use done policy and fall back at worst extra one. Done choose coupling iff k<r0-r1; pending choose coupling iff k<(r0-r1)(1-D_(t+1)). At last slot D_(T+1)=1: no repair benefit when full gradient pending, disable positive-cost coupling (ties arbitrary). Thus optimal verification protocol depends on formal learning communication state; no additional gradient variance or omitted samples. The conditional law proof applies regardless of optimality, provided decision pre-draft; DP optimum only known fixed rates, no hidden side info, no congestion, no unequal repair/upload cost, zero incremental payload time, no shared-resource contention. If rates depend on previous tokens, need expanded Markov state/estimated policy, not claim same two-state optimum. General V1 terminal g/b if different fallback cost, formula still holds but D may exceed1 and more repairs can be economically preferable; not tested. Same-info delegated flat and ordinary SD+batching retain learning law, do not require new HFL optimizer. No proof that same endpoint and transport transaction exists in real self-SD/HFL.
