## T: group gradient attenuation and geometric tilt
Fixed positive p_i,q_i, target frozen within draft step, away from ties, L=sum_i log(p_i(X_i)/q_i(X_i)), score s_i=grad log q_i. grad_i TV(P,Q)=-E_Q[1{L>0}s_i]. Define B_i=sum_v sqrt(p_i(v)q_i(v)), B=prod_i B_i, T_i(v)=sqrt(p_i(v)q_i(v))/B_i, T=prod T_i. Change of measure Q/T=B exp(-L/2). Exact gradient=-B E_T[1{L>0} exp(-L/2)s_i]. Thus scaled estimator is bounded by max score norm; exact gradient norm <= B max score norm, B potentially exponential in cohort size when affinities bounded below 1. This is an upper bound not universal strict attenuation rate; conventional group gradient may vanish. Conditional integrate lane i under T_i as in R71; exact means agree after restoring B; no general variance domination between Q-RB and T-RB. Factor B can be aggregated as sum log B_i; T samples local, sum of likelihood ratios broadcast. For logits the scaled per-lane estimator is q_i(v) G - T_i(v) w(v), w=1{S+r_i(v)>0} exp[-(S+r_i(v))/2], G=sum_v T_i(v)w(v). This is classical importance sampling/Chernoff tilt applied to group draft loss, not novel foundation. Removing B gives a per-cohort preconditioner, not unbiased original gradient, and mixing normalized gradients changes objective. Empirical norm matching computes TV gradient and rescaling, nonlinearity means no unbiased gradient claim; no convergence proof. Score bounded in finite fixed-feature heads, not arbitrary backbone.
