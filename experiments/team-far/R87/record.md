## T1 full route set certificate
Given candidate S of k nominal experts, box z_j in [-r_j,r_j], robust strict top-k set is equivalent to for all i in S,j outside S: a_i-a_j > sum_h r_h |B_ih-B_jh|. Proof: independent scalar uncertainty attains worst pairwise difference at a vertex, and all cross-set comparisons characterize strict top-k membership. Under ties, non-strict comparisons with deterministic tie order are needed for exact set equivalence. Complexity O(k(E-k)d); common offset cancels, tighter than separate independent score intervals by triangle inequality. All vertex top-k sets identical iff full route invariant under generic no ties, because each pairwise affine margin is minimized at a vertex. 
Partial membership: for i in S, count j!=i for which a_i-a_j <= sum r|B_i-B_j|; fewer than k implies i always selected, sufficient not necessary as pairwise adversaries may be mutually incompatible. 
## T2 exact mandatory membership under scalar uncertainty
For s_i(z)=a_i+b_i z, z in [-r,r], define outrank count C_i(z)=sum_j 1{s_j(z)>s_i(z) or equal and j has higher deterministic tie priority}. Expert i mandatory iff max_z C_i(z)<k. Each comparator changes only at at most one scalar crossing, so sort crossings and sweep interval counts, considering endpoints, one-sided intervals and exact tie point counts; O(E log E) per candidate, O(k E log E) for nominal k candidates (all other candidates cannot be mandatory since z=0 feasible). Code's random tests assume no ties at boundaries, and do not cover near-equal crossings/production FP; implementation not fully certified for endpoint ties (the initial open-interval count must be distinct from the left endpoint count). Exact mathematical algorithm handles them explicitly. Counterexample to pairwise count: s0=0,s1=z-.1,s2=-z-.1,z in [-1,1], k=2. Each competitor can outrank i=0 but never both, so i=0 always selected. For d>1, exact membership equivalent to feasibility of at least k simultaneous outranking inequalities (account ties); combinatorial cost and actual rank/bound acquisition unresolved. Not new general line sweep, robust optimization, or top-k theory; application distinction only.
## T3 late binding
At fixed x and known expert version compute f_i(x), preserve necessary local tape. After exact router completion, mix y=sum_{i in S} alpha_i f_i, alpha softmax restricted S. For upstream loss adjoint v, gradient wrt expert theta_i = alpha_i J_i^T v, wrt selected router score = alpha_i <v,f_i-y>. Identical to ordinary forward/backward away from top-k switches; router weight gradient follows chain rule. No expert VJP can generally be committed before v and alpha known. Equal route alone does not guarantee numerical output/gradient if x or expert version changed; if global router uses different normalization or auxiliary losses, those terms must be computed exactly too. No actual update trajectory tested.
